# Apology Email — To Startup Credits Providers

**From:** Nikhil — Founder, ClassGrid
**To:** MongoDB, AWS, Razorpay, Cloudflare, and all startup credits program teams
**Subject:** A sincere apology and thank you — from a founder who couldn't deliver

---

Dear Startup Credits Teams,

My name is Nikhil, and I was the founder of ClassGrid — an ed-tech platform for schools. I'm writing this email not as a business communication, but as an honest, personal apology from a founder who owes you one.

Over the past months, your companies generously extended startup credits to ClassGrid. MongoDB Atlas credits, AWS Activate credits, Razorpay's startup program, Cloudflare's startup plan — each of you believed in us enough to give us resources that cost real money, resources meant to help early-stage founders like me build something meaningful.

I failed to do that.

I want to be completely transparent: ClassGrid is shutting down. As of August 30, 2026, we have stopped operations. Our servers and database will be decommissioned on September 1, and all codebases will be deleted by September 2. The product I promised to build with your support — I could not turn it into what it needed to be.

This is not a form letter. This is me sitting with the uncomfortable truth that your teams reviewed our application, approved our credits, allocated resources to us — and I was not capable enough to make a product out of it. You trusted me with those credits, and I let that trust go to waste.

I know startup credits programs exist because your companies genuinely want to support builders. You don't owe founders anything — yet you extend these programs because you believe in the ecosystem. The fact that I demanded those credits, used them, and couldn't build something sustainable from them — that sits heavy on me.

I'm not writing this to ask for anything. I'm writing this because I believe the people behind these programs — the developer relations teams, the startup program managers, the people who read our applications and said "let's give this kid a shot" — deserve to hear directly from me that I'm sorry.

**What I owe you:**
- **MongoDB** — Thank you for the Atlas credits. I used them to build our school database infrastructure. I'm sorry I couldn't grow it into something lasting.
- **AWS** — Thank you for the Activate program credits. I used them to host our platform. I'm sorry the platform won't be running past this week.
- **Razorpay** — Thank you for the startup program support. I built our payment flow with your tools. I'm sorry we never processed enough transactions to justify your trust.
- **Cloudflare** — Thank you for the startup plan. You kept our site fast and secure. I'm sorry there won't be a site to protect anymore.
- **To every other company** that extended credits, tools, or support to ClassGrid — thank you, and I'm sorry.

I don't want to dress this up with "lessons learned" or "silver linings." The truth is simple: you gave, I received, and I couldn't deliver. That's on me.

If there are any outstanding balances, unused credits to formally release, or any administrative steps I need to take to properly close out our accounts with your programs, please let me know and I will handle them immediately.

Thank you for supporting founders — even the ones like me who don't make it. Your programs matter. The failure was mine, not yours.

With sincere gratitude and apology,

**Nikhil**
Incapable person who started ClassGrid
August 30, 2026
